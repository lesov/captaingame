Sailing a square-rigger

She cannot point closer than about 6 points (≈67°) to the wind; the red wedge on the rose shows the headings you cannot hold. Steer into it and the sails go aback: she loses way and drifts astern, in irons. With sternway the rudder works in reverse.

The shaded shape on the rose is her speed at each heading. She is fastest on a broad reach. To change tack, wear ship (turn through downwind) or tack through the wind with plenty of way on.

Gunnery of the day

Guns sit on broadside carriages and barely traverse, so you aim by turning the ship. Elevation is set with a wooden quoin under the breech, from about −3° to +10°.

Fired level (point blank) a ball first grazes the water near 370 m, and at a flat angle it skips on. At full quoin it carries about 2 km, though hitting anything out there is luck. The dashed arc on the water marks the first graze.

Heel matters. The lee battery points down and the weather battery up; in a stiff breeze the lee lower ports go awash and only half the guns bear. Guns fire in a ripple as the ship rolls.

Some ships also carry a bow or stern chaser, mounted on the centreline. It never loses bearing to heel, but it only bears dead ahead or dead astern — useful for plinking a chase before you can bring a broadside round.

Round shot breaks hulls. Chain shot cuts rigging and slows the enemy. Grape clears decks at short range. Reloading took minutes; here the clock runs ×3.
